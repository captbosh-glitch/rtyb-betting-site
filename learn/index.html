RTYB tracks the opening segment of every game. Here’s what each market means and how we grade it.

First-segment bets

Instead of the whole game, these bets settle on the first piece of it: the 1st period in the NHL, the 1st quarter or 1st half in football and basketball, and the 1st inning or first five innings in baseball. They settle fast and depend on how teams start, which is what RTYB’s records and trends measure.

Spread (ATS)

The favorite gives points and the underdog gets them: “Lakers −1.5” wins if the Lakers win the segment by 2 or more. Records are W-L-P — a push (P) is when the margin lands exactly on the line, and the bet is refunded. Spreads are usually priced at −110 (risk $110 to win $100) unless another price is listed.

Moneyline and SU

A moneyline bet is on who wins the segment outright. RTYB shows these records as SU (straight up), W-L-T. A tie in the segment is a push for the bet (0 units). Prices: −150 means risk $150 to win $100; +130 means risk $100 to win $130.

Totals (Over / Under)

A bet on combined points, goals or runs in the segment. Records are O-U-P. A half-point line (44.5) can’t push; a whole-number line (44) pushes when the total lands on it exactly.

NRFI / YRFI and First Five (F5)

NRFI is “no run first inning” — neither team scores in the 1st. YRFI is the opposite. There are no pushes; if the 1st inning isn’t finished, it’s No Action. First Five bets settle on the score after five innings: the F5 spread is always 0.5 runs (a tie goes to the +0.5 side), an F5 moneyline tie is a push, and fewer than five innings played means No Action.

Units

1 unit = $100 risked on every game in a category at the listed price. A loss is −1 unit; a win pays by the price (+0.91u at −110). Pushes, ties and No Action are 0. Records that need odds — spreads, totals, favorites and units — only count games with saved odds, and say how many (“based on N games with odds”).
